\subsection{System Overview}

This problem focuses on Arabic Information Retrieval (IR) and Question
Answering (QA) using classical keyword retrieval, dense semantic retrieval,
and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).  The entire system is built
around a single Arabic literary book as the sole knowledge source, allowing
a faithful comparison of how each technique handles the same queries.

% ── Selected Book ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Selected Book}

The selected book is \textit{\textarabic{الأيام}} (\textit{Al-Ayyam}, ``The Days'')
by \textit{\textarabic{طه حسين}} (Taha Hussein), one of the most celebrated
figures in modern Arabic literature.  The autobiography narrates Taha
Hussein's rural Egyptian childhood, his blindness, and his intellectual
journey through religious and secular education --- providing linguistically
rich, less-commonly indexed text that makes retrieval benefits clearly
visible.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Book Title:} \textarabic{الأيام} (\textit{Al-Ayyam})
  \item \textbf{Author:} \textarabic{طه حسين} (Taha Hussein)
  \item \textbf{Language:} Classical / Modern Standard Arabic
  \item \textbf{Source:} Public-domain Arabic text
\end{itemize}

% ── Preprocessing ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Preprocessing and Chunking}

The raw text underwent the following normalization steps:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Removal of unnecessary whitespace, line breaks, and page numbers
  \item Removal of Arabic diacritics (tashkeel) via Unicode range
  \item Character normalization:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textarabic{أ} / \textarabic{إ} / \textarabic{آ}
            $\rightarrow$ \textarabic{ا}
      \item \textarabic{ى} $\rightarrow$ \textarabic{ي}
      \item \textarabic{ة} $\rightarrow$ \textarabic{ه}
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

The normalized text was split into overlapping chunks:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Chunk size:} 4 sentences \quad
        \textbf{Overlap:} 2 sentences (50\,\% stride)
  \item Chunks with fewer than 100 Arabic characters were discarded
  \item Copyright / metadata patterns were filtered out
\end{itemize}

This yielded approximately \textbf{304 chunks} and
$\sim$1\,200 sentences from the full text.

% ── Embedding and Indexing ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Embedding and Indexing}

Dense vector representations were generated for every chunk using:
\begin{center}
  \texttt{sentence-transformers/paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2}
\end{center}
This model supports 50+ languages and was chosen for its strong Arabic
cross-lingual transfer performance.  All 768-dimensional embeddings were
$\ell_2$-normalized and stored in a FAISS
\texttt{IndexFlatIP} index, which provides exact cosine-similarity search
over unit-norm vectors.

% ── Language Model ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Language Model}

\begin{center}
  \texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}
\end{center}
This 1.5\,B-parameter instruction-tuned model was downloaded from
HuggingFace and executed entirely on CPU, satisfying the 1\,B--3\,B
local-execution constraint.  For RAG generation the model received
a structured Arabic system prompt together with the retrieved context;
for LLM-only answers it received only the query.

% ── Retrieval Methods ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Retrieval Methods}

\begin{description}
  \item[Classical Retrieval --- BM25 (Okapi BM25).]
        Each paragraph is tokenized into Arabic words; common stop-words
        and words shorter than two characters are removed.  Given a query,
        BM25 assigns a probabilistic relevance score that rewards term
        frequency in the paragraph while penalizing very frequent terms
        (IDF) and very long documents.  The 5 highest-scoring
        paragraphs are returned.  BM25 is completely insensitive to
        synonymy: a semantically rich passage that shares no surface tokens
        with the query always receives a score of zero.

  \item[Semantic Retrieval --- Embeddings + FAISS.]
        The query is fed through the same multilingual sentence-transformer
        used at index time.  Its embedding is compared against all 304
        stored embeddings via cosine similarity through FAISS, and the 5
        most similar chunks are returned.  This approach captures
        meaning and paraphrase, making it far more robust for indirect or
        descriptive Arabic questions.
\end{description}

% ── System Implementation ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{System Implementation}

The team built a complete, deployable end-to-end application from scratch
in two Python modules that integrate all of the above components.

\subsubsection{\texttt{main.py} --- Core Pipeline}

\texttt{main.py} implements the full pipeline in three stages that can be
run sequentially:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stage 1 --- Book Preparation:} loads the raw UTF-8
        text file, applies all normalization and chunking steps,
        generates 768-d embeddings in batches of 32, L2-normalizes
        them, and writes the FAISS index (\texttt{faiss\_index.bin}),
        the chunk list (\texttt{paragraphs.pkl}), and metadata to disk.

  \item \textbf{Stage 2 --- Search Layer:} implements and exposes
        \texttt{classical\_search()} and \texttt{semantic\_search()}
        functions.  \texttt{classical\_search} builds a BM25Okapi
        corpus from tokenized chunks (falling back to a TF-IDF
        implementation if \texttt{rank\_bm25} is unavailable);
        \texttt{semantic\_search} normalizes the query vector and
        calls FAISS.

  \item \textbf{Stage 3 --- RAG Generation:} the \texttt{rag\_answer()}
        function retrieves the top-5 semantically relevant passages,
        formats them into a numbered Arabic context block, and passes
        this to Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct via a structured chat template.
        In parallel it generates an LLM-only answer from the same
        model with no context, enabling direct side-by-side comparison.

  \item \textbf{Report Mode:} running \texttt{python main.py -{}-report}
        automatically runs all 10 queries through both retrieval paths and
        the RAG pipeline, then writes a complete Markdown deliverables
        report to \texttt{output/assignment\_report.md}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{\texttt{web\_ui.py} --- Interactive Web Interface}

\texttt{web\_ui.py} provides a full-featured, production-quality web
application built with Flask:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dark-theme Arabic-first UI:} a fully RTL
        (\textit{direction: rtl}) interface rendered in the
        \textit{Tajawal} Arabic typeface with a professional dark
        colour scheme, responsive on mobile.

  \item \textbf{Search tab:} accepts free-form Arabic queries, displays
        the top-$k$ results from BM25 and semantic search side by side
        in two styled columns with rank badges and colour-coded similarity
        scores.  Query terms are highlighted in the retrieved passages.
        One-click demo chips for direct, indirect, and hard questions
        allow instant testing.

  \item \textbf{Q\&A (RAG) tab:} submits the query to the RAG endpoint,
        displays the RAG answer, the LLM-only answer, and the supporting
        evidence context side-by-side with a loading indicator.  A
        180-second timeout guard prevents the browser from hanging on
        slow CPU inference.

  \item \textbf{REST API:} two endpoints (\texttt{/search} and
        \texttt{/ask\_rag}) are exposed for programmatic use;
        the RAG endpoint runs generation in a background thread to
        prevent Flask worker blocking.
\end{itemize}

The web application runs at \texttt{http://localhost:5010} and requires
no configuration beyond having the pre-built FAISS index in
\texttt{output/}.

\subsection{Comparison and Reflection}

\subsubsection{Classical vs.\ Semantic Retrieval}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Table: BM25 vs.\ Semantic retrieval --- quantitative summary}\\[4pt]
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|p{5.5cm}|X|X|}
\hline
\rowcolor{best}
\textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{BM25 (Classical)} & \textbf{Semantic (Embeddings)} \\
\hline
Best use case              & Short keyword queries   & Indirect / descriptive queries \\
\hline
Score range observed       & 0.06 -- 0.21            & 0.45 -- 0.77 \\
\hline
Average top-1 score        & $\approx 0.14$          & $\approx 0.65$ \\
\hline
Sensitivity to paraphrase  & None                    & High \\
\hline
Arabic morphology robustness & Low                  & High \\
\hline
Speed                      & Very fast               & Fast (via FAISS) \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Semantic retrieval consistently returned more relevant and contextually
meaningful results, especially for indirect and descriptive queries
(Queries 4, 6, 7, 9, 10) where the BM25 top-1 score was below 0.15
versus semantic scores above 0.65.  BM25 performed competitively only
for short keyword queries (1 and 2) where exact word overlap sufficed.
Arabic's morphological richness means that surface-form token matching
systematically under-performs for any query that does not
use the same root form as the indexed text.

\subsubsection{RAG vs.\ LLM-only Generation}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Table: RAG vs.\ LLM-only --- qualitative comparison across 10 queries}\\[4pt]
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|X|X|}
\hline
\rowcolor{best}
\textbf{Q} & \textbf{RAG Answer quality} & \textbf{LLM-only quality} \\
\hline
1  & Partially relevant (weak model) & Completely hallucinated \\
\hline
2  & Grounded in book description    & Confuses with religious context \\
\hline
3  & Relevant to book context        & Generic religious answer \\
\hline
4  & Correct and specific            & Refused to answer \\
\hline
5  & Accurate mythology description  & Unable to answer \\
\hline
6  & Precise, from source text       & Generic child psychology \\
\hline
7  & Accurate sibling relationship   & Refused / deflected \\
\hline
8  & Accurate cholera event          & Language-switched to Chinese \\
\hline
9  & Correct Quran-forgetting scene  & Lack of knowledge \\
\hline
10 & Accurate distinction            & Plausible but generic \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

RAG significantly improved answer quality across all 10 queries.
Without retrieval, the LLM:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hallucinated completely} unrelated content
        (Queries 1, 2, 3 --- confusing the book with Islamic topics)
  \item \textbf{Refused to answer} due to lack of context
        (Queries 4, 7, 9)
  \item \textbf{Switched languages mid-output} --- generating Chinese text
        for Query 8, a stark demonstration of the instability of small
        Arabic-language models
\end{itemize}

With RAG, grounding in retrieved passages produced answers that were
directly relevant to the book and factually consistent with the source
text, even though the 1.5\,B-parameter model occasionally introduced
minor inaccuracies in summarization.

\subsubsection{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Semantic retrieval is superior} for Arabic literary text.
        Arabic's rich morphology makes exact token matching unreliable;
        embedding-based retrieval bridges the gap across word forms,
        conjugations, and normalizations.

  \item \textbf{RAG drastically reduces hallucination.}  Providing even
        a small amount of retrieved context transformed the LLM's
        outputs from generic (often wrong) to specific and book-grounded.

  \item \textbf{Small LLMs remain a bottleneck.}  Even with high-quality
        retrieval, the 1.5\,B model produced mixed-language outputs and
        minor fabrications, indicating that generation quality at this
        scale is still fragile for Arabic.

  \item \textbf{Book topic choice amplifies the benefit.}
        \textit{Al-Ayyam} is a less commonly indexed title, so LLM-only
        answers were especially weak, making the RAG improvement
        particularly visible.
\end{enumerate}
